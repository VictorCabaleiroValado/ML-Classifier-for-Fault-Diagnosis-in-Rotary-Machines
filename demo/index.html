Can the computer recognize it?

The recording comes with a description of the problem. We keep that answer aside, let the computer make its own guess, then compare the two.

A closer look — if you’re curious

How did it reach that answer?

The computer follows a sequence of yes/no questions about numbers that describe the vibrations. This method is called a decision tree. Below is its exact route to the answer; the questions do not explain what physically caused the problem.

    What information does the computer use?

    The computer does not read the picture. It uses 72 numbers calculated from the full recording. These numbers describe things such as how large or spread out the vibrations are. They are called features.

    The reference is one recording, not a healthy population or an alarm threshold. Larger values do not necessarily mean a worse fault. Highlighted rows are used in the current decision.

    Evaluation loads with the model.

    Some training and test recordings may come from related experiments. That can make the task easier than recognizing a problem in a completely new machine. The recorded labels come from filenames; the computer disagreeing with a label does not prove the label is wrong.

    No calibrated confidence, fault severity, remaining-life prediction or maintenance recommendation is provided. The 50/75 RPM scenarios are synthetic transformations of 25 RPM sources. They do not verify the historical 50/75 RPM labels or reproduce measured operating speeds.

    What are these machine parts, and where do the data come from?

    A shaft is a rod that spins. Bearings support it and help it turn smoothly; small balls roll between an inner and an outer ring. The motor turns the system, and the gearbox transfers rotation through gears. Labels can describe one problem or several together.

    The Python workflow connects vibration measurements, time-domain and FFT features, supervised learning and evaluation. This browser demo uses the time-domain decision tree.

    The chart retains min/max points from 240 bins. Connecting lines do not reconstruct the full waveform. Sample indices and original sensor values are shown; sampling rate and physical units are not assumed. FFT is not computed from reduced display points.

    B1 / B2 identify the two bearings; X, Y and Z are their recorded axes. Motor, Gearbox and Tachometer complete the nine channels.

    Read the data and methodology notes ↗

    For 25 RPM, one example per category is selected by its lowest held-out row index. Synthetic scenarios use those same parents only, with inherited labels; they never enter model training. Downloaded signal values are checked against their feature vectors.
